#4471 · three problems, not one

owner Paul Robichaux proposed reviewed 2026-10-30 · 0 days ago

#4471 is marked known: one bug, one fix queued. Its reports say otherwise. Three different bugs were merged into it, because the desk merges on subject line (decision 0001), and every one of these reports has a subject like “wrong day”. The queued parser fix closes only the first.

Do not close #4471 when the parser fix ships. Two of its problems need fixes of their own.

The table reads the same export as #4471. It counts each report once, because the export pages overlap and some reports come twice.

parser: day-first dates read month first

Every tenant on file writes the day first. The shared parser reads every date month first, so a booking for 8 January becomes 1 August. This is the bug #4471 names, and the queued fix is for it.

hint: the booking-v3 form asks for the month first

The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. At a day-first tenant, people follow the hint and type the month first, and the form reads it the tenant’s way, day first. The dates swap just like the parser bug, so the reports read the same. They are a different bug: the parser fix does not change the hint. causes.ts already knew this cause; #4471’s cause table marks it “no ticket of its own”.

one-day: dates that move by one day

Some reports are not a swap at all. A Tuesday shows as a Monday. A client in Lisbon gets Tuesday, and a client in Toronto gets Monday, for the same meeting. An invite arrives before the booking was made. This looks like a time-zone shift: a date turned into a time, moved between zones, and read back as a date. The desk noted these reports as one-day moves (desk-notes.json), but #4471’s cause table still counts them under parser.

How the reports were placed

  • By the report first. A report the desk read as a one-day move is a one-day move, whatever its tenant.
  • Then by tenant and form, with causes.ts: a form report from a booking-v3 tenant is hint; any other report from a day-first tenant is parser.
  • Not by intake tag. Intake guesses parser or hint from the browser, and it disagrees with the tenant often.
  • Not by subject. The subjects are the same across all three problems.

Lessons

  • A subject line tells you what the reporter saw, not what broke. Read the body before you merge a report into a known ticket.
  • When a ticket’s own cause table shows a second cause, give that cause its own ticket.
